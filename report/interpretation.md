The comparison of multiple myeloma (MM) plasma cells with normal plasma cells (NPC)
revealed substantial transcriptional dysregulation. Several of the most significant
differentially expressed probes showed large changes in expression, including
IGHV3OR15-7, BIVM, IGLV9-49, LIMD1-AS1, GTF2A1 and RPS10. Functional enrichment 
analysis indicated that these expression changes were associated with processes 
involving cell adhesion, cell junction assembly, small GTPase-mediated signalling,
lymphocyte activation, mononuclear cell differentiation and cellular morphogenesis.
These findings suggest that the transcriptional differences between MM and normal
plasma cells are not restricted to individual genes but involve biological processes 
controlling how cells communicate, interact with their surroundings and regulate their cellular state.

  The KEGG analysis provides further support for this interpretation, with strong enrichment
of the PI3K-Akt signalling pathway, MAPK signalling pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor
interaction and calcium signalling pathway. These pathways are particularly relevant because
they participate in intracellular signalling, cellular survival, communication and regulation
of cellular behaviour. The enrichment of PI3K-Akt signalling is also consistent with the original
GSE47552 study, which discussed abnormalities in signalling and survival mechanisms during 
the progression of monoclonal gammopathies toward multiple myeloma. The original study also 
reported altered apoptosis-related genes and signalling processes, including PI3K/AKT-related mechanisms.
Therefore, although the exact top 10 genes from the present MM-versus-NPC analysis are not identical
to the genes highlighted in the original publication, the pathway-level results provide a biologically
meaningful connection between the present analysis and the published findings.

  Overall, these results suggest that the transition from normal plasma cells to malignant myeloma cells
is accompanied by coordinated changes in cell signalling, cell-cell interaction, immune regulation
and survival-related pathways. The GO enrichment for cell adhesion and small GTPase signalling, together 
with KEGG enrichment for PI3K-Akt, MAPK and cytokine signalling, suggests that altered communication
between cells and changes in intracellular signalling may contribute to the malignant plasma-cell
phenotype. These findings support the interpretation that the dysregulated genes identified in 
GSE47552 are involved in broader biological networks rather than acting independently. 
However, enrichment analysis demonstrates association with these pathways and does not by
itself establish that every pathway is activated; the direction and activity of individual
pathways would require further gene-level or experimental validation.
